Key takeaways

  • ✓Consistency: use the same accounting methods year after year; if a change is justified, disclose it and its effect.
  • ✓Conservatism (prudence): anticipate no profit, but provide for all known or probable losses, e.g. stock at the lower of cost or net realisable value.
  • ✓Disclosure: give all information that matters to users, often in notes to the accounts.
  • ✓Materiality: report separately only what is significant enough to affect decisions; it depends on the size and nature of the item.

Notes

The four conventions

Accounting conventions are widely accepted practices that make financial reporting regular and open. They sit alongside the accounting concepts as part of GAAP.

Consistency

Accounting methods are applied the same way over time, unless there is a valid reason to change.

Why
Users can compare performance across years.
Valid reasons to change
A law or accounting standard requires it, or it gives a more appropriate presentation.
Example
Changing from FIFO to weighted average stock valuation, or from straight-line to written-down-value depreciation, with the reason and effect disclosed. (LIFO is not permitted under AS 2 or Ind AS 2.)

Conservatism (prudence)

"Anticipate no profit, but provide for all possible losses." Gains are recognised when realised; known or probable losses are provided for.

Examples
Closing stock at the lower of cost or net realisable value; provision for doubtful debts
Note
Prudence should not be used to deliberately understate profits or create secret reserves.

Disclosure

All information that could affect users' understanding of the statements must be disclosed.

Usually through notes to the accounts: accounting policies, loan terms and repayment schedules, contingent liabilities. Companies must follow Schedule III and the Accounting Standards.

Materiality

Only items significant enough to influence users' decisions need separate reporting; trivial items can be grouped.

It depends on the item's size relative to the business and on its nature. ₹50,000 may be material for a small shop but not for a large company; some items, like fraud, matter whatever the amount.

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongConsistency means a method can never change.

RightIt can change for a valid reason, but the change and its effect must be disclosed.

WrongAny possible lawsuit loss is recorded as an expense.

RightRecord a provision only if payment is probable and measurable; if merely possible, disclose it.

WrongSwitching from FIFO to LIFO is a good consistency example.

RightLIFO is not permitted in India under AS 2 / Ind AS 2.

WrongMateriality is a fixed rupee limit.

RightIt depends on the item's size relative to the business and its nature.
