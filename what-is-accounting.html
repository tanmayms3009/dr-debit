Definition and features

Every business records its financial and money transactions through accounting. The owner needs a clear picture of profits or losses, the state of assets and liabilities, and the changes in equity over a period.

The best-known definition comes from the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants (AICPA):

"Accounting is the art of recording, classifying and summarising in a significant manner and in terms of money, transactions and events which are, in part at least, of a financial character, and interpreting the results thereof."

This definition points to five features of accounting.

It is an art

Applying accounting well needs specialised knowledge, experience and judgement.

It helps achieve financial goals by showing operating profit and financial position.

Record, classify, summarise

Recording
Writing each transaction in the books (journal)
Classifying
Grouping entries of a similar nature into ledger accounts
Summarising
Preparing reports from the ledger: income statement and balance sheet

In terms of money

Money is the common measure. Every item is recorded as an amount in ₹.

If it can't be measured in money, it isn't recorded.

Financial character

Only transactions and events of a financial nature are recorded.

A transaction involves another party (a sale). An event can be internal (a loss by fire).

Interpreting results

Reading the results to judge the business's financial status, progress and performance.

Accounting doesn't stop at the Balance Sheet.

SituationRecorded?Why

A competitor opens a shop next doorNoAffects the business, but has no money value to record
The owner buys a flat for the family from personal savingsNoNot a business transaction: the owner and the business are separate

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongAccounting and bookkeeping are the same.

RightBookkeeping is mainly recording. Accounting goes further: it also classifies, summarises and interprets.

WrongEvery important event is recorded.

RightOnly events measurable in money and of a financial character are recorded.

WrongOnly transactions with other parties are recorded.

RightThe definition says transactions and events. A loss by fire is an event, and it is recorded.

WrongQuoting the definition as "in a meaningful manner".

RightThe standard wording is "in a significant manner… in part at least, of a financial character".
