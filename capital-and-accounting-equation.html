Notes

Capital and the equation

Capital, or owner's equity, is the owner's share in the business. It is found by subtracting total liabilities from total assets. It is called residual interest because it is what remains for the owner once all obligations are settled.

The size of owner's equity helps judge a business's financial health and stability. It shows how much of the assets are financed with the owner's money rather than borrowed money.

Items of capital

Initial capital investment
The owner's first investment to set up the business
Additional capital
Further money or assets the owner brings in later
Retained earnings
Profits kept in the business instead of being taken out (in companies, not paid as dividends)
Revaluation surplus
The increase when assets are revalued upward
Drawings
Cash or goods the owner takes for personal use. These reduce capital.

The equation

Assets = Liabilities + Capital

Rearranged
Capital = Assets − Liabilities
Liabilities = Assets − Capital
Closing capital
Opening capital + Additional capital + Profit − Drawings (− Loss)
Why it always balances
Every transaction has two sides (double entry), so a change on one side is matched by an equal change.

Capital is the business's obligation to the owner, under the business entity concept.

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongCapital is the cash the business has.

RightCapital is the owner's claim on all assets: Assets − Liabilities. Cash is just one asset.

WrongDrawings are a business expense.

RightDrawings reduce capital directly. They don't appear in the Profit & Loss Account.

WrongBuying furniture for cash increases total assets.

RightOne asset rises and another falls by the same amount. Total assets don't change.

WrongCapital is not a liability in any sense.

RightUnder the business entity concept, it is what the business owes to its owner.
