Notes

How and when to record

Dual aspect

Every transaction has a two-fold effect of equal value: every debit has a corresponding credit.

This is the basis of the double-entry system and keeps Assets = Liabilities + Capital balanced.

Realisation (revenue recognition)

Revenue is recognised when goods are delivered or services are provided and there is reasonable certainty of payment.

Not when the order is placed, and not necessarily when cash is received. An advance from a customer is a liability (income received in advance) until delivery.

Accrual

Revenues and expenses are recognised in the period in which they occur, regardless of when cash is received or paid.

So the statements show all revenue earned and all expenses incurred in the period.

Matching

Expenses of a period are matched against the revenues of the same period to find the correct profit.

Examples: depreciation spreads an asset's cost over the years it helps earn revenue; cost of goods sold is matched with the sales of those goods.

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongRevenue is recorded when the order is received.

RightIt is recorded when goods are delivered (or services done) and payment is reasonably certain.

WrongAn advance from a customer is income.

RightIt is a liability (income received in advance) until the goods or services are delivered.

WrongOnly expenses actually paid count for the year.

RightUnder accrual, expenses incurred count, even if unpaid (outstanding).

WrongMatching means matching debits with credits.

RightThat is dual aspect. Matching sets a period's expenses against its revenues.
