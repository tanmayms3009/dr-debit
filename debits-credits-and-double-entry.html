The rules of debit and credit

To record transactions in the double-entry system, you need to know what debits and credits do. Every transaction affects at least two accounts. Debits and credits keep the accounting equation balanced: Assets = Liabilities + Capital.

Effect of debits and credits (modern approach)

Account typeDebit (Dr)Credit (Cr)Example of an increase
AssetsIncreaseDecreaseCash received → Dr Cash
ExpensesIncreaseDecrease (e.g. a refund)Rent paid → Dr Rent
DrawingsIncreaseDecreaseOwner withdraws cash → Dr Drawings
LiabilitiesDecrease (e.g. a loan repaid)IncreaseLoan taken → Cr Loan
CapitalDecrease (e.g. drawings, losses)IncreaseOwner invests → Cr Capital
RevenueDecrease (e.g. sales returns)IncreaseCommission earned → Cr Commission

A transaction can affect more than two accounts (a compound entry). The rule still holds: total debits = total credits.

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongDebit means bad, credit means good.

RightThey only mean left side and right side. Whether they increase or decrease depends on the account type.

WrongA "Cr" on my bank statement means money went out.

RightThe statement is the bank's record. A deposit increases what the bank owes you, so the bank credits it.

WrongDrawings are credited because capital goes down.

RightDrawings A/c is debited. The debit reduces capital.

WrongEvery entry has exactly one debit and one credit.

RightCompound entries can have several. Only the totals must match.
