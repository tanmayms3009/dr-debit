Key takeaways

30-second read
  • ✓Financial accounting gives reliable records, the basis for financial statements, and information for owners, managers, lenders and tax authorities.
  • ✓It helps compare years, plan budgets, settle legal disputes, file taxes (Income-tax, GST) and value a business.
  • ✓But it has limits: it records only money items, relies on estimates and judgement, uses historical cost, and ignores inflation.
  • ✓Accounts can also be manipulated (window dressing). Use them alongside other information.

Notes

Strengths and limits

Advantages

Systematic records
Transactions are recorded carefully, so nothing depends on memory, which matters more as a business grows.
Basis for financial statements
Accurate records allow the Trading and P&L Account and Balance Sheet to be prepared.
Analysing results
This year's results can be compared with earlier years to spot important changes.
Decision-making
Supports planning, budgets and coordination of departments.
Legal evidence
Properly kept records can be used as evidence in legal proceedings.
Information for stakeholders
Owners, creditors, managers, staff, customers and government departments get the information they need.
Taxation
Tax authorities rely on the accounts for Income-tax and GST.
Valuing a business
Accounting information helps fix a fair price when a business is sold.

Limitations

Only money terms
Events that cannot be measured in money, such as staff skills or reputation, are left out.
Estimates
Some figures depend on estimates, like an asset's useful life or likely bad debts, which may not be accurate.
Personal judgement
Accountants choose between methods for stock valuation, depreciation and capital vs revenue items, so reported profit can vary.
Historical cost
Fixed assets stay at their original cost, so the Balance Sheet may not show current values.
Possible manipulation
Figures can be manipulated to look better than reality (window dressing), so accounts alone may not measure management performance accurately.
Price-level changes ignored
Money's value changes over time, but accounts treat it as stable, so inflation is not reflected.

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongThe Balance Sheet shows what a business would sell for.

RightIt shows mostly historical cost and leaves out unrecorded items like brand, so market value can be very different.

WrongAccounting is completely objective.

RightIt relies on estimates and choices of method, which affect profit.

WrongAccounts help with sales tax returns.

RightSales tax was replaced by GST in 2017. Accounts support Income-tax and GST.

WrongBecause of its limitations, accounting is unreliable.

RightIt is reliable within its rules; just read it alongside other information.
