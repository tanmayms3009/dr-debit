Goods, Debtors & Creditors

6 slides Quiz: Medium · High · Expert

Key takeaways

30-second read
  • ✓Goods are items a business buys or makes in order to sell. Unsold goods are inventory, a current asset.
  • ✓Whether something is "goods" depends on the business: a laptop is goods for a laptop shop, but an asset for a law firm.
  • ✓Debtors (accounts receivable) owe us money for credit sales: a current asset.
  • ✓Creditors (accounts payable) are suppliers we owe for credit purchases: a current liability.

Notes

Goods, receivables and payables

Goods

Tangible items a business buys or makes to sell to customers. Central to any retail or manufacturing business.

Raw materials
Basic inputs for production, like wood for furniture or flour for bread
Work in progress (WIP)
Partly finished items, including all costs so far
Finished goods
Completed products ready for sale
In the accounts
Unsold goods are inventory, a current asset. Once sold, their cost moves to cost of goods sold (COGS).

Debtors (accounts receivable)

Customers who received goods or services on credit and still owe the business. A current asset, usually collected within a year.

Invoices
Bills sent to customers that set out what they owe
Credit terms
When payment is due, e.g. "net 30" = within 30 days of the invoice
Ageing report
Groups dues by how long they're outstanding, to judge collectability
Bad debts
Amounts that won't be collected. Businesses also estimate a provision for doubtful debts.
Collections & write-offs
Following up for payment; writing a debt off as a loss once collection fails

Creditors (accounts payable)

Suppliers the business owes for goods and services already received. A current liability.

Invoices
The supplier's bill; the amount owed is recorded as a creditor
Payment terms
Due date, and sometimes a discount for early payment, e.g. "2/10, net 30"
Ageing of payables
Groups bills by age to plan cash and prioritise payments
Supplier relationships
Paying on time builds trust and can win better credit terms
Payment management
Scheduling payments to protect cash flow and use early-payment discounts

Watch out

Outstanding expenses
Expenses incurred but unpaid, like salary or rent due, are shown as outstanding expenses, a separate current liability. They are not trade creditors.
Goods depend on the business
Cars are goods for a car dealer, but an asset for a taxi company.
Account type
Debtors and creditors are persons, so they are Personal accounts.

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongDebtors are a Real account because they're an asset.

RightDebtors are persons, so they're a Personal account, even though they're an asset.

WrongUnpaid salary is shown under creditors.

RightIt is an outstanding expense, a separate current liability.

WrongAnything a business buys is "goods".

RightOnly items bought or made to sell. Items for use are assets.

WrongA bad debt is simply forgotten.

RightIt is written off as a loss, reducing debtors and profit.
