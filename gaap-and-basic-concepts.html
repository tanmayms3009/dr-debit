GAAP and five basic concepts

Generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP) are widely accepted rules that promote uniformity, dependability and comparability in financial statements. Investors, analysts and lenders rely on them to compare businesses fairly. They cover issues such as when to recognise revenue and how to classify items in the balance sheet.

In India, they take the form of Accounting Standards (AS) formulated through the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India, and Ind AS notified by the Central Government for specified companies. GAAP is commonly divided into accounting concepts and accounting conventions.

Business entity

The business is treated as separate from its owner for accounting purposes.

Effect
Transactions are recorded from the business's point of view. Capital is shown as owed to the owner; personal withdrawals are drawings.
Note
This is an accounting separation. A sole proprietorship or partnership is not a separate legal entity; a company is.

Money measurement

Only transactions and events that can be measured in money are recorded.

Not recorded
Reputation, staff skills, customer loyalty
Limitation
It ignores changes in money's value (inflation, exchange rates), so statements may not show current economic values.

Accounting period

The life of a business is divided into equal periods for measuring results.

Usual period
One year. In India, the financial year runs from 1 April to 31 March.
Why
Owners, lenders and tax authorities need results regularly, not only when the business ends.

Cost

Assets are recorded at their historical cost, the price paid plus costs to make them ready for use.

Why
Cost is objective and verifiable; market values are subjective and change.
Exceptions
Assets may be formally revalued (creating a revaluation surplus), and Ind AS allows fair value for some items.

Going concern

The business is assumed to continue in the foreseeable future, unless there is evidence otherwise.

Effect
Assets are shown at cost less depreciation, spread over their useful life, not at what they would fetch if the business closed. Prepaid expenses are carried forward.
If the business is closing
The assumption no longer holds, and assets are valued at realisable value.

Concepts vs conventions

Concepts
Basic assumptions: business entity, money measurement, accounting period, cost, going concern, dual aspect, realisation, accrual, matching
Conventions
Accepted practices: consistency, conservatism, disclosure, materiality

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongUnder the business entity concept, a sole proprietorship is a separate legal entity.

RightIt is separate only for accounting. Legally, the owner and business are the same; only companies are separate legal entities.

WrongAssets are shown at their current market value.

RightUnder the cost concept they are shown at historical cost (less depreciation), unless formally revalued.

WrongGoing concern means the business will never close.

RightIt is an assumption for the foreseeable future, dropped if there is evidence the business will close.

WrongAccounts adjust old asset values for inflation.

RightMoney measurement ignores changes in money's value. That is one of its limitations.
