Notes

Reading a balance sheet

The balance sheet is a snapshot of a business's assets, liabilities and capital at a particular moment. With the income statement and cash flow statement, it is one of the main reports for judging financial status. In India it is prepared under the Accounting Standards (AS or Ind AS) and, for companies, the format in Schedule III of the Companies Act, 2013.

Horizontal format

Used by sole traders and partnerships. Two sides, side by side.

Left: Liabilities
Capital (+ net profit − drawings), long-term loans, current liabilities (creditors, bills payable, outstanding expenses)
Right: Assets
Non-current assets (land, buildings, machinery, furniture), current assets (stock, debtors, cash and bank)

Vertical format (companies)

Schedule III, Companies Act 2013. One column, top to bottom.

I. Equity and Liabilities
Shareholders' funds, non-current liabilities, current liabilities
II. Assets
Non-current assets, current assets

What it shows

Liquidity
Whether current assets are enough to pay current liabilities
Creditworthiness
Creditors and banks judge whether they will be repaid and how risky lending is
Progress over time
Comparing balance sheets of several years shows how the financial position has changed
How it is funded
How much of the assets are financed by borrowing versus the owner's money

Key ideas

Equation
Assets = Liabilities + Capital (for companies: shareholders' equity)
Date
"As at 31 March", because it is a position on one date
A statement, not an account
It has no debit or credit sides and does not use "To" and "By"

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongHeading it "for the year ended 31 March".

RightA balance sheet is "as at 31 March". "For the year" is for the income statement.

WrongShowing net profit as a separate income item.

RightNet profit is added to capital; drawings are deducted from it.

WrongWriting "To" and "By" in a balance sheet.

RightIt is a statement, not an account, so it has no To/By or Dr/Cr sides.

WrongA profitable business can always pay its bills.

RightCheck current assets vs current liabilities. Profit and liquidity are different things.
