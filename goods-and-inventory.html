Key takeaways

30-second read
  • ✓Goods are items a business buys or makes in order to sell.
  • ✓Whether something is goods depends on the business: a laptop is goods for a laptop shop, but an asset for a law firm.
  • ✓A manufacturer's goods pass through three stages: raw materials → work in progress → finished goods.
  • ✓Unsold goods are inventory, a current asset. Once sold, their cost becomes cost of goods sold.

Notes

Goods and inventory

Goods are tangible items a business acquires for the purpose of selling them to customers. They are central to any retail or manufacturing business. Managing inventory well matters for both cash flow and profit: too much stock ties up cash, too little loses sales.

Categories of goods

Raw materials
Basic inputs for production, like wood for furniture or flour for bread
Work in progress (WIP)
Partly finished items, valued at all costs incurred so far
Finished goods
Completed products ready for sale

A trader holds only goods bought for resale. A manufacturer can hold all three.

In the accounts

Unsold goods
Shown as inventory (stock), a current asset in the Balance Sheet
Goods sold
Their cost moves to cost of goods sold in the income statement
Formula
COGS = Opening stock + Purchases − Closing stock
Gross profit
Sales − COGS

It depends on the business

The same item can be goods for one business and an asset for another.

Goods
Bought or made to sell: cars for a car dealer, laptops for a laptop shop
Asset
Bought to use for years: a taxi company's cars, a law firm's laptops

Goods that leave without a sale

Taken by the owner
Treated as drawings at cost, not as a sale: Drawings A/c Dr, To Purchases A/c
Lost by fire or theft
A loss at cost: Loss by Fire A/c Dr, To Purchases A/c
Given as free samples
An advertising expense at cost: Advertisement A/c Dr, To Purchases A/c

Goods or not?

ItemClassification
Timber in a furniture factory's storeGoods raw materials
Half-built cupboards on the factory floorGoods work in progress
Cars on a car dealer's showroom floorGoods finished goods
The car the dealer's manager uses for workNon-current asset, not goods
Notebooks on a stationery shop's shelvesGoods bought for resale
Paper used in a law firm's officeExpense (office supplies), not goods

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongAnything a business buys is "goods".

RightOnly items bought or made to sell. Items for use are assets or expenses.

WrongCost of goods sold equals purchases.

RightAdjust for stock: Opening stock + Purchases − Closing stock.

WrongGoods taken by the owner are recorded as sales.

RightThey are drawings at cost. No sale happened.

WrongUnsold goods at year end are an expense.

RightThey are inventory, a current asset, until sold.
