Key takeaways

  • ✓Financial statements (final accounts) are the end product of accounting. They answer two questions: the result for a period and the position at its end.

Notes

What the statements are

Financial statements, often called final accounts, are the results of the financial accounting process. They summarise the accounts to find out two things: the net profit or loss from trading for a period, and the state of the business's finances (assets and liabilities) at the end of that period.

Income statement

Trading and Profit & Loss Accounts. Includes all revenues and gains, and all expenses and losses, for the period.

Answers: did we make a profit or a loss?

Balance sheet

The position statement. Shows assets, liabilities and capital at the end of the period.

Answers: what do we own and owe, and how is it funded?

Cash flow statement

Shows cash coming in and going out from operating, investing and financing activities over a period.

Answers: where did cash come from and go? Helps judge liquidity, solvency and flexibility.

Who prepares what

Sole traders & partnerships
Usually a Trading Account, a Profit & Loss Account and a Balance Sheet
Companies (Companies Act, 2013)
Balance Sheet, Statement of Profit and Loss, Cash Flow Statement, Statement of Changes in Equity (where applicable) and Notes. Some small companies and one person companies need not prepare a cash flow statement.
Rules in India
Accounting Standards (AS) or Ind AS issued through ICAI, and Schedule III for company formats

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongAll debit balances are expenses.

RightDebit balances are assets or expenses and losses. Machinery has a debit balance but is an asset.

WrongThe Balance Sheet shows the profit for the year.

RightProfit is found in the income statement; it is then added to capital in the Balance Sheet.

WrongFinal accounts are prepared straight from the journal.

RightJournal → ledger → trial balance → final accounts.

WrongEvery business must prepare a cash flow statement.

RightSole traders usually don't, and some small companies are exempt.
