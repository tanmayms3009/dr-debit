# Voice-over script: Debtors & Creditors

Lesson page: `debtors-and-creditors.html`  
Length: about 1:50. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:08)

You sell goods worth fifty thousand rupees today. But your bank balance doesn't move at all. So where's the money? Let's find out.

## Slide 2 · The credit cycle (0:08–0:25)

Most businesses buy and sell on credit. You buy goods from a supplier and pay later: that supplier is your creditor. You sell to a customer who pays later: that customer is your debtor. Debtors and creditors are mirror images.

## Slide 3 · Debtors (0:25–0:50)

Debtors owe us, so they're a current asset. An invoice gives us the right to be paid. Credit terms say when: net thirty means within thirty days of the invoice. An ageing report sorts dues by how long they're unpaid. And if a customer can't pay, the amount is a bad debt, written off as a loss.

## Slide 4 · Creditors (0:50–1:12)

Creditors are suppliers we owe for goods and services already received, so they're a current liability. Payment terms tell us when to pay, and some suppliers offer a discount for paying early. One catch: unpaid expenses like salary due are outstanding expenses, a separate liability, not trade creditors.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:12–1:38)

Let's see both. We sell goods for fifty thousand on net thirty terms. That customer is now a debtor for fifty thousand. We also buy goods for thirty thousand on terms of two by ten, net thirty: two percent off if we pay within ten days. We pay on day eight, so we pay twenty-nine thousand four hundred and save six hundred.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:38–1:50)

So where's the fifty thousand? With your customer. They're your debtor until they pay. Remember: debtors owe us, so they're an asset. Creditors are owed by us, so they're a liability. And both are personal accounts.
