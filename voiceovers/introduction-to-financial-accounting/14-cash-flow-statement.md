# Voice-over script: The Cash Flow Statement

Lesson page: `cash-flow-statement.html`  
Length: about 1:42. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

A business made a healthy profit this year. Yet next week, it can't pay its staff salaries. How can a profitable business run out of cash? The cash flow statement explains it.

## Slide 2 · What it is (0:10–0:22)

The cash flow statement tracks every rupee of cash coming in and going out during a period. It's one of the main financial statements, alongside the income statement and balance sheet. In India, it follows Accounting Standard 3, or Ind AS 7.

## Slide 3 · Three activities (0:22–0:42)

Every cash flow fits one of three groups. Operating: the day-to-day business, like cash from customers and cash paid for expenses. Investing: buying or selling long-term assets, like machinery, or investments. Financing: money from or back to owners and lenders, like loans, capital and dividends.

## Slide 4 · Profit is not cash (0:42–1:00)

Why does profit differ from cash? Credit sales count as profit before any cash arrives. Buying machinery uses cash but isn't an expense right away. Repaying a loan uses cash but isn't an expense at all. And depreciation reduces profit without using any cash.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:00–1:28)

Here's a year. Operating activities brought in one lakh ten thousand. Investing used one lakh fifty thousand on new machinery. Financing brought in sixty thousand: a loan of one lakh, less drawings of forty thousand. Net increase in cash: twenty thousand. Opening cash one lakh ten thousand, so closing cash is one lakh thirty thousand.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:28–1:42)

So how does a profitable business run out of cash? Customers haven't paid yet, cash went into new machinery, or loans were repaid. That's why owners watch the cash flow statement alongside profit. Profit keeps a business alive in the long run. Cash keeps it alive this week.
