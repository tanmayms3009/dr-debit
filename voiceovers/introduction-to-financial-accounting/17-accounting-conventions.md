# Voice-over script: Accounting Conventions

Lesson page: `accounting-conventions.html`  
Length: about 1:58. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

Your closing stock cost one lakh rupees. But prices have fallen, and now it would sell for only eighty thousand. So what value goes in the books? Cost, or the lower selling value?

## Slide 2 · Consistency (0:10–0:35)

Conventions are accepted practices for preparing accounts. The first is consistency. Use the same methods every year, so this year can be compared with last year. You can change a method if a law or standard requires it, or it gives a better picture. For example, moving from FIFO to weighted average stock valuation. But you must disclose the change and its effect.

## Slide 3 · Conservatism (0:35–1:02)

Conservatism, or prudence: anticipate no profit, but provide for all known or probable losses. So closing stock is valued at the lower of cost or net realisable value. And a provision is made for debts that may not be collected. But a lawsuit loss is recorded only if payment is probable and can be estimated. If it's merely possible, it's disclosed as a contingent liability instead.

## Slide 4 · Disclosure and materiality (1:02–1:25)

Disclosure: share everything that matters to users, usually through notes to the accounts. Like the terms of a loan, or a pending lawsuit. Materiality: only items significant enough to affect decisions need separate reporting. A small stapler is simply an expense. And materiality depends on the business. Fifty thousand rupees matters to a small shop, but not to a large company.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:25–1:48)

Let's value the stock item by item. Item A cost forty thousand and would sell for fifty thousand: take the lower, forty thousand. Item B cost sixty thousand but would sell for only forty thousand: take forty thousand. Closing stock is eighty thousand. Notice we ignored the possible gain on A, but recognised the loss on B. That's conservatism.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:48–1:58)

So, the stock? It goes in at eighty thousand, the lower value. That's conservatism. Remember the four conventions: consistency, conservatism, disclosure and materiality.
