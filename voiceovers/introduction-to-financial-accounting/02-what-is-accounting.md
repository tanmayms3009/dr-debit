# Voice-over script: What Is Accounting? Definition & Features

Lesson page: `what-is-accounting.html`  
Length: about 1:55. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:08)

Your best employee resigns. It's a huge loss for the business. So, does it go into the accounts? Hold that thought. By the end of this lesson, you'll know exactly why the answer is no.

## Slide 2 · The definition (0:08–0:30)

Here's the classic definition, from the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants. Accounting is the art of recording, classifying and summarising, in a significant manner and in terms of money, transactions and events which are, in part at least, of a financial character, and interpreting the results thereof. Learn it word for word. Exams love it.

## Slide 3 · Four stages (0:30–0:55)

That definition hides four stages. First, recording: every transaction goes into the journal. Second, classifying: similar entries are grouped into ledger accounts. Third, summarising: the ledger becomes a trial balance, a profit and loss account and a balance sheet. Fourth, interpreting: reading those results to judge how the business is doing.

## Slide 4 · Example (0:55–1:25)

Let's follow one transaction. Rent of fifteen thousand rupees is paid in cash. We record it in the journal: rent account debit, to cash account. We classify it by posting to the rent account in the ledger. At year end, total rent of one lakh eighty thousand is summarised in the profit and loss account. And we interpret it: rent is fifteen percent of income of twelve lakh rupees. Is that too high?

## Slide 5 · Two filters (1:25–1:45)

Before anything is recorded, it must pass two filters. Can it be measured in money? And does it have a financial character? Goods sold for twenty thousand rupees: yes and yes, so it's recorded. A manager resigning: no amount of money, so it's not recorded. That answers our opening question.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:45–1:55)

So, is the resignation recorded? No. It can't be measured in money. Remember the four stages: record, classify, summarise, interpret. And the two filters: money and financial character. That's accounting.
