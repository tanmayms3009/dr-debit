# Voice-over script: Capital & the Accounting Equation

Lesson page: `capital-and-accounting-equation.html`  
Length: about 1:42. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

A business owns things worth ten lakh rupees. It owes outsiders four lakh. So how much of this business really belongs to the owner? That number has a name, and it sits at the heart of all accounting.

## Slide 2 · Capital (0:10–0:25)

That number is capital, also called owner's equity. It's what's left for the owner after every outsider has been paid. Assets minus liabilities. Because it's what remains at the end, it's also called residual interest.

## Slide 3 · The equation (0:25–0:42)

Rearrange it and you get the accounting equation. Assets equal liabilities plus capital. Everything the business owns was paid for either by outsiders or by the owner. It must balance after every single transaction.

## Slide 4 · What moves capital (0:42–1:00)

What changes capital? It goes up when the owner invests at the start, adds more money later, when profits are kept in the business, and when assets are revalued upward. It goes down when the owner takes drawings, or when the business makes a loss.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:00–1:30)

Watch the equation stay balanced. The owner starts with five lakh cash: assets five lakh, capital five lakh. Buy goods for two lakh on credit: assets go to seven lakh, liabilities two lakh. Withdraw thirty thousand for personal use: assets six lakh seventy thousand, capital four lakh seventy thousand. Balanced every time.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:30–1:42)

Back to our question. Owns ten lakh, owes four lakh. Six lakh belongs to the owner. That's capital. And remember the equation: assets equal liabilities plus capital. Always.
