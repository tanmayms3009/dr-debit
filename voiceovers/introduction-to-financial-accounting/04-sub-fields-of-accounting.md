# Voice-over script: Sub-fields of Accounting

Lesson page: `sub-fields-of-accounting.html`  
Length: about 1:52. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:12)

A bakery owner has three questions. Did we make a profit last year? What does one cake really cost us? And should we open a second shop next year? Three questions, and each one is answered by a different branch of accounting.

## Slide 2 · Five sub-fields (0:12–0:35)

Here are the five sub-fields. Financial accounting looks at the past and reports to outsiders. Management accounting helps managers plan and decide. Cost accounting works out and controls costs. Social responsibility accounting measures the business's impact on society. And human resource accounting puts a value on people.

## Slide 3 · The big comparison (0:35–0:55)

The big comparison. Financial accounting looks back at the past, follows fixed rules and standards, and reports mainly to outsiders once a year. Management accounting looks ahead, has no fixed format, and gives managers reports whenever they need them.

## Slide 4 · Example (0:55–1:20)

Now cost accounting in action. The bakery makes two thousand cakes. Materials cost sixty thousand rupees, labour thirty thousand and overheads ten thousand: one lakh in total. So each cake costs fifty rupees. But it's sold for forty-five. That's a loss of five rupees on every cake, and only cost accounting reveals it.

## Slide 5 · Beyond the usual accounts (1:20–1:40)

Two newer fields measure what normal accounts leave out. Social responsibility accounting weighs the social costs and benefits of a firm's actions, like pollution against jobs created. Human resource accounting measures the investment in people, such as training, which traditional accounts treat as just an expense.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:40–1:52)

Back to the bakery. Profit last year: financial accounting. Cost of one cake: cost accounting. Should we open a second shop: management accounting. Different questions, different branches, one business.
