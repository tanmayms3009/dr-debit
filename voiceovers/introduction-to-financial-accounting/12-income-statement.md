# Voice-over script: The Income Statement

Lesson page: `income-statement.html`  
Length: about 1:42. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

A shop earns two lakh twenty thousand rupees of profit on the goods it sells. But at the end of the year, its profit is only eighty thousand. Where did the rest go? The income statement shows you, step by step.

## Slide 2 · What it is (0:10–0:25)

The income statement lists all revenues and gains, and all expenses and losses, for a period. The difference is profit or loss. You'll hear it called the profit and loss account, statement of operations, or earnings statement. Companies call it the statement of profit and loss.

## Slide 3 · Two parts (0:25–0:45)

For a trading business, it has two parts. The trading account deals with goods: sales, minus the cost of goods sold, gives gross profit. Then the profit and loss account takes gross profit, adds other incomes, subtracts indirect expenses like salaries and rent, and gives net profit.

## Slide 4 · Direct vs indirect (0:45–1:05)

How do you know where an expense goes? Direct expenses are linked to buying or making goods: wages, carriage inwards, factory power. They go to the trading account. Indirect expenses are about running and selling: office salaries, rent, advertising, carriage outwards. They go to the profit and loss account.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:05–1:30)

Here are the numbers. Sales six lakh. Cost of goods sold three lakh eighty thousand. Gross profit two lakh twenty thousand. Add commission received, ten thousand. Subtract indirect expenses of one lakh fifty thousand: salaries, rent, advertising and depreciation. Net profit: eighty thousand.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:30–1:42)

So where did the rest go? The two lakh twenty thousand was gross profit, after only the cost of goods. Running the business, salaries, rent, advertising and depreciation, used up a lot of it. What's left is net profit: eighty thousand.
