# Voice-over script: Debits, Credits & Double Entry

Lesson page: `debits-credits-and-double-entry.html`  
Length: about 1:45. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

You deposit ten thousand rupees in your bank. Your bank statement says: credit, ten thousand. Wait, doesn't credit mean money going out? Let's clear this up for good.

## Slide 2 · Double entry (0:10–0:25)

First, double entry. Every transaction touches at least two accounts. One side is debited, the other credited, and the totals always match. Debit simply means the left side of an account. Credit means the right side. That's all.

## Slide 3 · The modern rules (0:25–0:40)

The modern rules. Debit increases assets, expenses and drawings. Credit increases liabilities, capital and revenue. To decrease any account, do the opposite. Remember it as DEA and LCR.

## Slide 4 · Same answer, two ways (0:40–1:02)

Do these clash with the golden rules? Never. Take rent paid in cash. Modern rules: expense goes up, so debit rent. Cash, an asset, goes down, so credit cash. Golden rules: rent is a nominal expense, so debit it. Cash is real and goes out, so credit it. Same entry.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:02–1:28)

Three transactions. The owner invests three lakh in cash: debit cash, credit capital. Machinery bought on credit for one lakh twenty thousand: debit machinery, credit the supplier. Commission of fifteen thousand received in cash: debit cash, credit commission. Total debits, four lakh thirty-five thousand. Total credits, the same.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:28–1:45)

Back to the bank. Your statement is the bank's record, not yours. To the bank, your deposit is money it owes you, a liability. Liabilities increase with a credit. In your own books, your bank account is an asset, so you debit it. Same money, two points of view.
