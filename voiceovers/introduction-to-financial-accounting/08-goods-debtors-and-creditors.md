# Voice-over script: Goods, Debtors & Creditors

Lesson page: `goods-debtors-and-creditors.html`  
Length: about 1:52. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

Two businesses each buy a laptop. One is a laptop shop. The other is a law firm. Is the laptop goods, or an asset? Here's the twist: it's different for each of them.

## Slide 2 · Goods (0:10–0:30)

Goods are items a business buys or makes in order to sell. For a manufacturer, they move through three stages. Raw materials, like wood or flour. Work in progress, things half made. And finished goods, ready to sell. Until they're sold, goods sit in the balance sheet as inventory, a current asset.

## Slide 3 · Debtors (0:30–0:52)

Debtors are customers who've received goods or services but haven't paid yet. They owe us, so they're a current asset. Credit terms say when to pay. Net thirty means within thirty days of the invoice. And if a customer can't pay, the amount becomes a bad debt and is written off as a loss.

## Slide 4 · Creditors (0:52–1:15)

Creditors are the flip side. They're suppliers we owe for goods and services already received. So they're a current liability. Payment terms tell us when to pay, and some suppliers offer a discount for paying early. One catch: unpaid expenses like salary due are outstanding expenses, a separate liability, not trade creditors.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:15–1:40)

Let's see both. We sell goods for fifty thousand on net thirty terms. That customer is now a debtor for fifty thousand. We also buy goods for thirty thousand on terms of two by ten, net thirty: two percent off if we pay within ten days. We pay on day eight, so we pay twenty-nine thousand four hundred and save six hundred.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:40–1:52)

So, the laptop? For the laptop shop, it's goods, bought to sell. For the law firm, it's an asset, used for years. And remember: debtors owe us, so they're an asset. Creditors are owed by us, so they're a liability.
