# Voice-over script: Financial Statements: The Big Picture

Lesson page: `financial-statements-big-picture.html`  
Length: about 1:42. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

A business records thousands of transactions in a year. The owner has five minutes. How do they see the whole year at a glance? That's the job of financial statements.

## Slide 2 · The main statements (0:10–0:30)

Financial statements, also called final accounts, summarise the year. The income statement shows the result: profit or loss for the period. The balance sheet shows the position: assets, liabilities and capital at the end. And the cash flow statement shows how cash came in and went out.

## Slide 3 · The journey (0:30–0:42)

Where do they come from? Transactions are recorded in the journal, posted to the ledger, and balanced. The balances are listed in a trial balance. And from the trial balance, we prepare the final accounts.

## Slide 4 · Where each balance goes (0:42–1:05)

Every balance in the trial balance has a home. Debit balances are either assets, which go to the balance sheet, or expenses and losses, which go to the income statement. Credit balances are either liabilities and capital, which go to the balance sheet, or incomes and gains, which go to the income statement.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:05–1:30)

Let's sort some balances. Machinery, debit: an asset, so balance sheet. Purchases, debit: an expense, so income statement. Capital, credit: balance sheet. Sales, credit: income, so income statement. Creditors, credit: a liability, so balance sheet. Commission received, credit: income statement.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:30–1:42)

So how does the owner see the year in five minutes? Through financial statements. The income statement for the result, the balance sheet for the position, and the cash flow statement for cash. All built from the trial balance.
