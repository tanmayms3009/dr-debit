# Voice-over script: Users of Accounting Information

Lesson page: `users-of-accounting.html`  
Length: about 1:42. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

A company publishes its accounts. Who actually reads them? The owner, sure. But also the bank, the tax department, its own employees, even its customers. And each of them is looking for something different.

## Slide 2 · Two groups (0:10–0:25)

Users fall into two groups. Internal users are inside the organisation: owners, managers and employees. External users are outside it: lenders, investors, the government, regulators, customers and the public.

## Slide 3 · Internal users (0:25–0:45)

Inside the business: owners want to know how their investment is performing. Managers need reports to plan, control and run things smoothly. And employees watch the profits, because their pay, bonus and job security depend on them.

## Slide 4 · External users (0:45–1:10)

Outside the business: lenders and suppliers ask, can they repay us? Investors ask, should we invest? The government and regulators check taxes and compliance with the law. And long-term customers want to know the business will be around to keep supplying them.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:10–1:30)

Here's one set of numbers. Profit of twelve lakh rupees, loans of eight lakh, total assets of forty lakh. The bank looks at the loans against the assets. The tax department looks at the profit. Employees see the profit and think bonus. Same numbers, different questions.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:30–1:42)

To recap: internal users are inside the business, and managers get detailed reports through management accounting. External users are outside, and they rely on the published financial statements. Same numbers, many readers.
