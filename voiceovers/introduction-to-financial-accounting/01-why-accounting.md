# Voice-over script: Why Businesses Need Accounting

Lesson page: `why-accounting.html`  
Length: about 1:45. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:08)

Imagine your stall sold forty thousand rupees of goods this month. Feels great, right? But here's the real question: did you actually make a profit?

## Slide 2 · Resources (0:08–0:25)

Every business runs on resources: money, materials, people's labour and machines. These resources are limited. How well they're used decides whether the business succeeds. So owners must keep a close eye on them.

## Slide 3 · Two questions (0:25–0:50)

To manage those resources, owners need answers to two big questions. One: what is the result? Did we make a profit or a loss? That's the Profit and Loss Account. Two: what is our position? What do we own, what do we owe, and where did the money come from? That's the Balance Sheet.

## Slide 4 · Example (0:50–1:15)

Let's see it in action. A small café earns one lakh twenty thousand rupees in a month and spends ninety-five thousand. The result: a profit of twenty-five thousand. Its position: it owns equipment and cash worth two lakh forty thousand, owes a supplier thirty thousand, and the owner's funds make up the rest, two lakh ten thousand.

## Slide 5 · The solution (1:15–1:35)

How do you get these answers reliably? By recording every business transaction in a systematic way. That's bookkeeping and accounting. And because business keeps getting more complex, the rules, called accounting standards, keep being updated.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:35–1:45)

So remember: resources go in, records keep track, and you get two answers. The result, in the Profit and Loss Account. And the position, in the Balance Sheet. That's why every business needs accounting.
