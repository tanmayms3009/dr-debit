# Voice-over script: Assets & Liabilities

Lesson page: `assets-and-liabilities.html`  
Length: about 1:45. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

You buy a laptop for your business for sixty thousand rupees, but you'll pay the shop next month. So what do you have now? An asset? A liability? Let's find out.

## Slide 2 · Assets (0:10–0:30)

An asset is a resource the business owns or controls, and that will bring benefits in the future. Some assets you can touch: machinery, furniture, vehicles, computers. These are tangible. Others you can't touch but still have value: goodwill, patents, trademarks and copyrights. These are intangible.

## Slide 3 · Liabilities (0:30–0:42)

A liability is what the business owes to outsiders and must pay in future. Buy goods on credit, and you owe the supplier. Borrow from a bank, and you owe the loan plus interest.

## Slide 4 · Split by time (0:42–1:05)

Both assets and liabilities are split by time. Current means settled within twelve months, or within the business's normal operating cycle. Cash, stock and debtors are current assets. Creditors and outstanding salary are current liabilities. Everything longer term is non-current, like machinery or a five-year bank loan.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:05–1:30)

Let's sort a real list. Cash fifty thousand, stock one lakh twenty thousand and debtors eighty thousand: current assets of two lakh fifty thousand. Machinery four lakh and a patent one lakh: non-current assets of five lakh. Creditors seventy thousand and outstanding salary twenty thousand: current liabilities of ninety thousand. And a five-year loan of three lakh: non-current.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:30–1:45)

Back to the laptop. It's both. The laptop is a non-current asset, used for years. The sixty thousand you owe the shop is a current liability, due next month. One transaction, two sides. That's how accounting always works.
