# Voice-over script: Dual Aspect, Realisation, Accrual & Matching

Lesson page: `dual-aspect-realisation-accrual-matching.html`  
Length: about 1:40. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

You deliver a one lakh rupee order to a customer in March. They pay you in April. Your financial year ends on thirty-first March. So which year's income is it? This year's, or next year's?

## Slide 2 · Dual aspect (0:10–0:25)

First, dual aspect. Every transaction has two equal effects. Borrow fifty thousand, and cash goes up fifty thousand while the loan goes up fifty thousand. That's why every debit has a matching credit, and why assets always equal liabilities plus capital.

## Slide 3 · Realisation (0:25–0:45)

Realisation tells you when to record revenue. Not when the order is placed. Not when the cash arrives. It's when the goods are delivered or the service is done, and payment is reasonably certain. And if a customer pays in advance, that's not revenue yet. It's a liability until you deliver.

## Slide 4 · Accrual and matching (0:45–1:05)

Accrual says: record revenue when it's earned and expenses when they're incurred, whatever the timing of cash. Matching says: set each period's expenses against that same period's revenue. Together, they give the true profit for the year. That's why March salary paid in April still counts in March.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:05–1:30)

The year ends thirty-first March. Services worth one lakh delivered in March, paid in April: count it this year. March salary of thirty thousand, paid in April: count it this year. Rent of forty-five thousand paid in March for April to June: not this year's expense, it's prepaid. Profit from these: seventy thousand. On a pure cash view, you'd see minus forty-five thousand.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:30–1:40)

So, the March delivery? It's this year's income, because it was earned in March. That's realisation and accrual. The cash arriving in April doesn't change which year earned it.
