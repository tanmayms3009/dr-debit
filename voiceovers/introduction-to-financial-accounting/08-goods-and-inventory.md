# Voice-over script: Goods & Inventory

Lesson page: `goods-and-inventory.html`  
Length: about 1:32. Record one take per slide, matching the slides on the lesson page.

## Slide 1 · Hook (0:00–0:10)

Two businesses each buy a laptop. One is a laptop shop. The other is a law firm. Is the laptop goods, or an asset? Here's the twist: it's different for each of them.

## Slide 2 · Goods (0:10–0:28)

Goods are items a business buys or makes in order to sell. So it's the purpose that decides, not the item. Cars are goods for a car dealer, but an asset for a taxi company. Furniture is goods for a furniture shop, but an asset for a bank.

## Slide 3 · Three stages (0:28–0:45)

A manufacturer's goods pass through three stages. Raw materials, like wood or flour. Work in progress, things half made. And finished goods, ready to sell. A trader skips the first two: it buys finished goods and sells them on.

## Slide 4 · Inventory to expense (0:45–1:02)

Until goods are sold, they're inventory, a current asset in the balance sheet. When they're sold, their cost moves to the income statement as cost of goods sold. The formula: opening stock, plus purchases, minus closing stock.

## Slide 5 · Example (1:02–1:20)

Let's try it. Opening stock forty thousand. Purchases two lakh. Closing stock sixty thousand. So cost of goods sold is one lakh eighty thousand. If sales were two lakh fifty thousand, gross profit is seventy thousand.

## Slide 6 · Recap (1:20–1:32)

So, the laptop? For the laptop shop, it's goods, bought to sell. For the law firm, it's an asset, used for years. Remember: unsold goods are inventory, and only goods actually sold become cost of goods sold.
