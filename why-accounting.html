Key takeaways

30-second read
  • ✓A business uses limited resources (money, materials, labour, machines) to earn a profit for its owners.
  • ✓Owners need two answers: the result (profit or loss?) and the position (what it owns, what it owes, and where the money came from).
  • ✓The result is shown in the Profit & Loss Account. The position is shown in the Balance Sheet.
  • ✓Bookkeeping and accounting give those answers by recording every transaction systematically, using generally accepted principles.

Notes

The idea, step by step

A business is an economic activity carried on to earn a profit for its owners. It can be run by one person (a sole proprietorship), by partners (a partnership) or as a company. It works within trade rules, social values and laws, both in India and across borders.

To run, a business needs resources: money, materials, labour and machines. These are limited, so how well they are managed decides whether the business succeeds. That is why owners must keep a close watch on them.

The result

Did the business make a profit or a loss?

Shown in
Profit & Loss Account
Covers
A period, e.g. the year ended 31 March
Built from
Nominal accounts: incomes and expenses

Profit = Income − Expenses

The position

What does it own and owe? Where did the money come from?

Shown in
Balance Sheet
Covers
One date, e.g. as on 31 March
Built from
Real and Personal accounts: assets, liabilities, capital

Owns = Owes + Owner's funds

The method

Record every transaction, systematically

Bookkeeping
Recording transactions accurately
Accounting
Classifying, summarising and interpreting them
Rules
Generally accepted principles and accounting standards, revised as business grows more complex

Accounting is treated as a branch of social science.

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongHigh sales means the business is profitable.

RightProfit = income − expenses. High sales with higher expenses is a loss.

WrongThe Balance Sheet shows the year's profit.

RightProfit or loss comes from the Profit & Loss Account. The Balance Sheet shows the position on one date.

WrongOnly big companies need accounting.

RightEvery business, even a one-person shop, needs records to know its result and position.

WrongAccounting rules are fixed and never change.

RightStandards are revised regularly as business becomes more complex.
