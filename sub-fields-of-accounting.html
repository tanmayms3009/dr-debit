Notes

The five sub-fields

As business grew more complex, accounting split into specialised fields. Each serves a different purpose and a different group of users.

Financial accounting

Prepares financial statements, mainly for external users.

Nature
Historical: records past transactions
Output
Profit & Loss Account and Balance Sheet
Purpose
Find the result for a period, and the financial position on a date, following set rules

Management accounting

Internal reporting for managers.

Helps with
Stewardship, planning, control and decision-making
Method
Organising and presenting information to suit what managers need
Uses
Draws heavily on cost accounting data

Cost accounting

Measures and controls the costs of production and operations.

ICMA, London (now CIMA) defines it as "the process of accounting for costs which begins with the recording of income and expenditure or the bases on which they are calculated and ends with the preparation of periodical statements and reports for ascertaining and controlling costs."

Social responsibility accounting

Computes and interprets the social costs and benefits of a firm's actions.

Grew as people paid more attention to the harm economic activity can do to society and the environment.

Human resource accounting

Identifies, measures and reports the investment in people.

Traditional accounts do not show employees' value. This field values human capital so it can be managed better.

Other fields

Tax accounting
Working out taxable income and taxes due under the Income-tax Act and GST, and preparing returns
Forensic accounting
Investigating fraud and financial disputes, often to provide evidence for courts

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongFinancial accounting is only for outsiders.

RightIt is prepared mainly for external users, but owners and managers use it too.

WrongManagement accounting follows a fixed legal format.

RightIt has no fixed format. Reports are designed around what managers need.

WrongCost accounting just records costs.

RightIt also ascertains and controls costs, as its definition says.

WrongEmployees appear as assets in a traditional Balance Sheet.

RightThey don't. That gap is exactly what human resource accounting tries to fill.
