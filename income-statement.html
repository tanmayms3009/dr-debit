Key takeaways

30-second read
  • ✓The income statement shows revenues, expenses and the resulting profit or loss for a period. It is also called the Profit & Loss Account, statement of operations or earnings statement.
  • ✓The Trading Account deals with buying, making and selling goods and finds gross profit.
  • ✓The Profit & Loss Account takes gross profit, adds other incomes, deducts indirect expenses, and finds net profit.
  • ✓Owners, managers, investors and lenders use it to judge profitability and control costs.

Notes

Trading and Profit & Loss

The income statement shows revenues, expenditures and the net income of a business over a period. Its main aim is to show financial performance: whether the business is making a profit or a loss. Together with the balance sheet and cash flow statement, it gives a full view of the business.

Trading Account

Gross profit = Net sales − Cost of goods sold

Net sales
Sales − Sales returns
Cost of goods sold
Opening stock + Net purchases + Direct expenses − Closing stock
Direct expenses
Wages, carriage inwards, freight inwards, factory power and fuel, factory rent

Profit & Loss Account

Net profit = Gross profit + Other incomes − Indirect expenses and losses

Other incomes
Commission received, interest received, rent received, discount received
Indirect expenses
Office salaries, office rent, advertising, carriage outwards, depreciation, interest on loans, bad debts
If expenses are larger
The result is a net loss

Why it matters

Profitability analysis
Shows whether profit can be improved by raising revenue, cutting costs, or both, and how well strategies worked
Expense tracking
Reveals rising or unexpected costs such as salaries, rent and supplies, and where budgets are overshot
Overall evaluation
Investors and lenders use it to judge performance and viability before investing or lending

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongGross profit and net profit are the same.

RightGross profit is after the cost of goods only. Net profit is after all expenses.

WrongCarriage outwards goes to the Trading Account.

RightCarriage inwards (bringing goods in) → Trading. Carriage outwards (delivering to customers) → P&L.

WrongCost of goods sold = purchases.

RightAdjust for opening and closing stock and add direct expenses.

WrongThe income statement is prepared "as on" a date.

RightIt covers a period: "for the year ended 31 March".
