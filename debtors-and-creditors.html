Debtors & Creditors

6 slides Quiz: Medium · High · Expert

Key takeaways

30-second read
  • ✓Debtors (accounts receivable) owe us for goods or services sold on credit. They are a current asset.
  • ✓Creditors (accounts payable) are suppliers we owe for credit purchases. They are a current liability.
  • ✓Credit terms such as "net 30" set when payment is due; early-payment discounts reward paying quickly.
  • ✓Unpaid expenses like salary due are outstanding expenses, a separate liability, not creditors.

Notes

Receivables and payables

Debtors (accounts receivable)

Customers who received goods or services on credit and still owe the business. A current asset, usually collected within a year.

Invoices
Bills sent to customers; they establish the right to receive payment
Credit terms
When payment is due, e.g. "net 30" = within 30 days of the invoice
Ageing report
Groups dues by how long they're outstanding, to judge collectability and credit risk
Bad debts
Amounts that won't be collected. Businesses also estimate a provision for doubtful debts.
Collections & write-offs
Following up for payment; writing a debt off as a loss once collection efforts fail

Creditors (accounts payable)

Suppliers the business owes for goods and services already received. A current liability.

Invoices
The supplier's bill; the amount owed is recorded as a creditor
Payment terms
Due date, and sometimes a discount for early payment, e.g. "2/10, net 30"
Ageing of payables
Groups bills by age to plan cash and prioritise payments
Supplier relationships
Paying on time builds trust and can win better credit terms
Payment management
Scheduling payments to protect cash flow, use early-payment discounts and avoid late penalties

Debtors vs creditors

BasisDebtorsCreditors
WhoCustomers who bought on creditSuppliers we bought from on credit
Money flowsTo usFrom us
Balance SheetCurrent assetCurrent liability
Account typePersonalPersonal
DiscountDiscount allowed (a loss to us)Discount received (a gain to us)
Main riskCustomer doesn't pay → bad debtPaying late → penalties, lost trust

Watch out: expenses incurred but unpaid, like salary or rent due, are shown as outstanding expenses, a separate current liability, not as trade creditors.

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongDebtors are a Real account because they're an asset.

RightDebtors are persons, so they're a Personal account, even though they're an asset.

WrongUnpaid salary is shown under creditors.

RightIt is an outstanding expense, a separate current liability.

Wrong"Net 30" means a 30% discount.

RightIt means the full amount is due within 30 days of the invoice.

WrongA bad debt is simply forgotten.

RightIt is written off as a loss, reducing debtors and profit.
