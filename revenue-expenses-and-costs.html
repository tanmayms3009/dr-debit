Key takeaways

30-second read
  • ✓Revenue is income from normal operations, like sales. It increases equity, but money the owner puts in is not revenue.
  • ✓Expenses are costs used up to earn revenue: operating, cost of goods sold, non-operating, depreciation and interest.
  • ✓A cost is what you pay to acquire or make something. It becomes an expense when it is used up or sold.
  • ✓Costs are classified as direct or indirect, fixed or variable. Opportunity cost guides decisions but is not recorded.

Notes

Revenue, expenses and costs

Revenue

Income from normal operations: the gross inflow of economic benefits in a period that increases equity, other than contributions from owners.

Sources
Sales, interest, royalties, dividends
When recognised
When goods are delivered or services are provided, and payment is probable (not necessarily received)
Why it matters
It is the top line of the income statement. Profit = Revenue − Expenses.

Expenses

Costs incurred in operations to earn revenue. They reduce assets (assets used up) or increase liabilities (more owed).

Operating expenses
Selling: advertising, sales commission. Administrative: office staff salaries, office supplies, insurance.
Cost of goods sold (COGS)
Materials, labour and overheads directly linked to the goods actually sold
Non-operating
Not part of core operations, e.g. losses on investments
Depreciation & amortisation
Spreading an asset's cost over its useful life: depreciation for tangible assets, amortisation for intangible ones
Interest expense
The cost of borrowed funds, such as interest on loans or debentures

Costs

The money value spent to acquire, produce or maintain goods and services, including delivery to customers.

Direct
Easily traced to one product, service or project: raw materials, direct labour
Indirect
Needed for operations but not traceable to one item: utilities, rent, administration
Fixed
Stay the same in total within a normal range of output: rent, salaries, insurance. Fixed cost per unit falls as output rises.
Variable
Change in proportion to output: raw materials, direct labour
Opportunity cost
The benefit given up when one option is chosen over another. Used for decisions; not recorded in the books.

Cost vs expense

A cost becomes an expense when the benefit is used up.

Stock bought
Cost held as an asset → becomes COGS when sold
Machinery bought
Cost held as an asset → becomes an expense year by year through depreciation
Rent for this month
Used up in the month → an expense straight away

Common mistakes

Where marks are lost

WrongRevenue is recorded only when cash is received.

RightIt is recognised when goods are delivered or services provided, and payment is probable.

WrongCapital brought in by the owner is income.

RightOwner contributions increase capital, not revenue.

WrongFixed costs never change.

RightThey stay fixed in total only within a normal range of output. Per unit, they fall as output rises.

WrongOpportunity cost is entered in the books.

RightIt is a decision tool only. No money is paid, so nothing is recorded.
